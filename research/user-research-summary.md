# User Research Summary

## Research focus

The interviews explored how UQ students discover clubs, what makes comparison difficult, and what information or product features could make joining decisions easier.

## Main themes

### 1. Discovery was not the only problem

Students already used several channels to find clubs, including Market Day, Instagram, friends, and the UQ Union directory. The larger challenge was deciding which option was worth joining.

### 2. Information was fragmented

Students described having to move between different platforms to compare clubs. Important information could be split across social-media bios, posts, stories, Facebook pages, Discord servers, or separate websites.

### 3. Practical details influenced decisions

Participants wanted concrete details such as:

- membership cost
- meeting or event frequency
- location
- what happens at events
- how to join
- whether beginners are welcome
- whether students can attend alone

These details were more useful for decision-making than general descriptions such as “friendly community” or “fun events.”

### 4. Uncertainty could stop follow-through

Some students remained interested after discovering a club but did not take the next step because the joining process was unclear or because they felt uncertain about attending alone.

### 5. Comparison tools reduced effort

Search and category filters helped users narrow the club list and supported the project goal of making comparison more efficient.

## Design implications

The research supported several product decisions:

- keep search and filtering as core functionality
- present structured club information for easier comparison
- provide practical, decision-relevant details rather than purely promotional content
- make joining steps clearer
- use the profile area to provide more useful feedback about a user's club choices
