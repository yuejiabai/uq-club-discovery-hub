# Learning Cards Summary

## Learning Card 1 — Practical details matter

**Initial assumption:** Club names, categories, and short descriptions would be enough for students to judge fit.

**Observed:** Students still wanted practical details such as cost, location, meeting time, event frequency, and what participation is actually like.

**Learning:** Discovery alone is not enough. Students need trustworthy information that supports comparison and confident choice.

**Product decision:** Retain structured club cards and detailed views, while improving the depth and reliability of club information.

---

## Learning Card 2 — Search and filters reduce browsing effort

**Initial assumption:** Search and category filters would make browsing easier.

**Observed:** Participants successfully used them to narrow the list and found this easier than checking multiple separate pages.

**Learning:** Filtering is core to the value proposition because students often begin with a rough interest area already in mind.

**Product decision:** Keep search and category filtering as core MVP features.

---

## Learning Card 3 — The profile should provide feedback

**Initial assumption:** Showing a list of joined clubs would make the profile flow feel complete.

**Observed:** The feature made the MVP feel functional, but the profile page remained limited in value.

**Learning:** The profile can do more than store joined clubs; it can help users reflect on interests and future choices.

**Product decision:** Explore a Club Preference Summary on the profile page. The selected React component in this portfolio reflects that experiment.
